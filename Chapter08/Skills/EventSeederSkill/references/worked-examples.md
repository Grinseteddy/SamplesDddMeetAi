# Worked examples

Three seeds, plus a naming clinic. Each shows the input *as it actually arrived* —
vague, incomplete, in the user's own words — so the gap between input and output
is visible. That gap is the skill.

Note what is absent from all three: no commands, no aggregates, no actors, no
bounded contexts. Just stickies.

## 1. Coarse seed from three sentences

**Input, verbatim:** *"We rent out cargo bikes in the city. People book on the
app, pick the bike up from a station, ride it, drop it back. We charge by the
hour. Sometimes bikes break and we have to go fix them."*

That is three sentences and one aside. It's enough.

### What I understood

Cargo-bike rental, from a rider wanting a bike to the rental being paid for.
Trigger: a rider looks for a bike. Outcome: the rental is closed and charged.
**Coarse** — several parts of the business are in play and no single process was
named.

### Events

1. Bike searched for
2. Bike reserved
3. Rental started
4. Bike unlocked at station
5. Bike ridden
6. Bike returned to station
7. Rental ended
8. Rental charged
9. Receipt sent

### Alternatives & failures

Generated by sweeping the six hiding places. The input mentioned only breakage;
everything else came from the sweep.

- Reservation expired *(branches from 2 — time passing)*
- Rental cancelled by rider *(2–3 — reversal)*
- Station reported lock fault *(4 — the outside world)*
- Rental extended by support *(5 — human judgement)*
- Battery fell below service level *(5 — threshold)*
- Bike reported broken *(5–6 — from the input)*
- Bike collected for repair *(after breakage)*
- Bike returned to fleet *(after repair)*
- Payment method declined *(8 — rejection)*
- Station found full on return *(6 — rejection)*

### Made up

Events 1, 4 and 9 in full — the input says "book on the app" and "pick the bike
up", not that searching, unlocking and receipts are distinct facts. The split
between *Bike reserved* (2) and *Rental started* (3) is mine. "Station found full
on return" assumes stations have capacity, which the input never says.

### Questions

- When does the clock actually start — reservation, unlock, or first movement?
- Are reservation and rental two things or one?
- Who notices a broken bike: the rider, staff, or the bike itself?
- What happens when a rider arrives at a full station?
- Is the charge taken at the end, or is money held at booking?

**What to notice:** nine spine events from three sentences, and *more*
alternatives than the input contained. The generated failures are what the room
will spend its time on.

## 2. Fine seed from a bulleted process

**Input, verbatim:** *"Claims process: customer submits claim with photos →
we check the policy is valid → adjuster reviews → approve or reject → if
approved, payout goes out within 5 days. Fraud team sometimes pulls a claim out
for a deeper look."*

### What I understood

A motor claim from submission to settlement. Trigger: customer submits a claim.
Outcome: the claim is settled, paid or rejected. **Fine** — one process, described
step by step, so every meaningful step gets its own sticky.

### Events

1. Claim submitted
2. Photos attached to claim
3. Policy validity checked
4. Claim assigned to adjuster
5. Claim reviewed
6. Repair estimate received
7. Claim approved
8. Payout scheduled
9. Payout released
10. Claim closed

### Alternatives & failures

- Claim rejected *(5–7 — rejection)*
- Claim partially approved *(7 — the input's "approve or reject" hides this)*
- Policy found lapsed *(3 — rejection)*
- Additional evidence requested *(5 — human judgement)*
- Evidence deadline passed *(after the request — time passing)*
- Claim escalated to fraud review *(4–5 — from the input)*
- Claim cleared by fraud team *(after escalation)*
- Claim confirmed fraudulent *(after escalation)*
- Payout failed *(9 — the outside world)*
- Payout recalled *(after 9 — reversal)*
- Payout deadline missed *(8–9 — the "within 5 days" promise, time passing)*
- Claim reopened *(after 10 — reversal)*

### Made up

Events 4, 6, 8 and 10 — the input names neither assignment, nor estimates, nor
scheduling, nor a closing step; I inferred them from "adjuster reviews" and
"payout goes out". *Claim partially approved* and *Payout deadline missed* are
mine, and both are guesses I'd bet on.

### Questions

- Does the 5-day clock keep running during fraud review?
- Is a rejected claim appealable — and is an appeal a new claim or a reopen?
- Who decides there's enough evidence, and what happens when there isn't?
- Is "approve or reject" really binary in practice?
- Does anyone get told when the payout fails?

**What to notice:** twelve alternatives against ten happy-path events. That ratio
is normal and is most of the value — the description was all happy path.

## 3. Seed from a domain story

**Input, verbatim:** a domain story the team had already written —

> 1. Visitor selects Plan on Pricing page
> 2. Visitor enters Payment details into Checkout
> 3. Billing system charges Payment details
> 4. Billing system creates Subscription for Visitor
> 5. System sends Welcome email to Customer

### What I understood

Self-service subscription signup. Trigger: a visitor picks a plan. Outcome: an
active subscription and a welcomed customer. **Fine** — the story is already
step-by-step. Each story sentence becomes a fact; the interesting work is
everything the single happy path couldn't express.

### Events

1. Plan selected
2. Payment details entered
3. Payment authorised
4. Subscription started
5. Customer welcomed

### Alternatives & failures

- Payment declined *(3 — rejection)*
- Card expired *(3 — time passing)*
- Checkout abandoned *(2 — time passing)*
- Plan changed before checkout *(1–2 — reversal)*
- Signup blocked as duplicate *(4 — rejection)*
- Welcome email bounced *(5 — the outside world)*
- Trial period ended *(after 4 — time passing)*
- Subscription cancelled within cooling-off period *(after 4 — reversal)*
- Renewal charged *(after 4 — time passing)*
- Renewal payment failed *(after renewal — rejection)*

### Made up

Everything after *Customer welcomed* — the story stops at signup, but a
subscription that never renews isn't a subscription, so the lifecycle events are
mine. *Trial period ended* assumes a trial exists, which the story doesn't
mention at all.

### Questions

- Is there a trial, and does signup mean "charged now" or "charged later"?
- Does the story's step 5 ("Welcome email") matter to the business as *notified*,
  or only as *email sent*? Named it `Customer welcomed` on that bet.
- Is cancellation immediate or at period end? The difference is an extra event.
- What is the business fact when the same person signs up twice?

**What to notice:** a domain story converts almost one-to-one into events, then
the six hiding places double the wall. Note also step 5's rename — the story's
`sends Welcome email` is plumbing; the fact is that the customer was welcomed.

## 4. Naming clinic

| Given as | Reads as | Rename to | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Create order | a command | Order placed | intentions aren't facts |
| Order created | CRUD | Order placed | the business word is "placed" |
| User updated | CRUD | Delivery address corrected | name *what* changed, and why |
| Processing payment | in-flight | Payment authorised | events are completed facts |
| Submit button clicked | UI | Claim submitted | the wall is not a screen |
| Record written to ledger | technical | Payment posted | domain language |
| Order handled | vague | Order picked · Order packed · Order dispatched | one vague event hides three real ones |
| Email sent | plumbing | Customer notified of dispatch | name the business intent, not the transport |
| Nightly job ran | mechanism | Overdue invoices flagged | the job is *how*; the flagging is *what* |
| Order not found | *legitimate* | Order not found | keep it — failure events are real facts |
