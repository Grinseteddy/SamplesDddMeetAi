# Worked examples

Three seeds at three granularities, plus a naming clinic. Each shows the *input
as it actually arrived* — vague, incomplete, in the user's own words — so the
gap between input and output is visible. That gap is the skill.

## 1. Big Picture seed from three sentences

**Input, verbatim:** *"We rent out cargo bikes in the city. People book on the
app, pick the bike up from a station, ride it, drop it back. We charge by the
hour. Sometimes bikes break and we have to go fix them."*

That is three sentences and one aside. It's enough.

### Scope

Cargo-bike rental, from a rider wanting a bike to the rental being paid for.
Trigger: a rider searches for a bike. Outcome: the rental is closed and charged.
**Big Picture** granularity — the ask ("seed a workshop") is coarse, several
functions are in play (booking, stations, maintenance, billing), and no one
process was named. Commands and aggregates are deliberately *not* generated.

### Event timeline

1. Bike searched for
2. Bike reserved
3. ⟂ **Rental started** *(pivotal — the business is now on the hook for a bike in the wild)*
4. Bike unlocked at station
5. Bike ridden
6. Bike returned to station
7. ⟂ **Rental ended** *(pivotal — value delivered; everything after is money and upkeep)*
8. Rental charged
9. Receipt sent

### Alternative & failure events

Generated by sweeping the six hiding places — note that the input mentioned only
one of these (breakage), and the other five came from the sweep:

| Source | Event | Branches from |
|---|---|---|
| Time passing | Reservation expired | 2 |
| Rejection | Payment method declined | 8 |
| External system | Station reported lock fault | 4 |
| Reversal | Rental cancelled by rider | 2–3 |
| Human decision | Rental extended by support | 5 |
| Threshold | Battery fell below service level | 5 |
| *(from the input)* | Bike reported broken · Bike collected for repair · Bike returned to fleet | 5–6 |

### Candidate bounded contexts

Marked as candidates — the seams are the two pivotal events plus the maintenance
cluster hanging off the side of the timeline:

- **Booking** (1–2, Reservation expired, Rental cancelled)
- **Rental** (3–7, Rental extended)
- **Billing** (8–9, Payment method declined)
- **Fleet & maintenance** (Bike reported broken → Bike returned to fleet, Battery fell below service level, Station reported lock fault)

### For the session

- **Assumptions to confirm:** that reservation and rental are two things and not
  one; that "Bike unlocked" is separate from "Rental started" (it may be the same
  moment, or hours apart); that charging happens after return rather than a hold
  at booking.
- **Questions for the room:** when exactly does the clock start — reservation,
  unlock, or first movement? Who bears the cost when a rider returns to a full
  station? Is a broken bike found by the rider, by staff, or by telemetry?
- **Hotspots:** "sometimes bikes break and we have to go fix them" hides an entire
  maintenance process with its own actors; the input has no station-capacity
  concept at all, yet returning implies one.

**What to notice:** nine spine events from three sentences, and *more*
alternative events than the input contained. The generated failures are what the
room will spend its time on.

## 2. Process-level seed with slices

**Input, verbatim:** *"Claims process: customer submits claim with photos →
we check the policy is valid → adjuster reviews → approve or reject → if
approved, payout goes out within 5 days. Fraud team sometimes pulls a claim out
for a deeper look."*

### Scope

Motor claim from submission to payout. Trigger: customer submits a claim.
Outcome: claim settled (paid or rejected). **Process-level** — the user is
describing one process end to end and wants to build against it, so the full
grammar is derived.

### Event timeline

1. Claim submitted
2. Photos attached to claim
3. Policy validity checked
4. Claim assigned to adjuster
5. Claim reviewed
6. ⟂ **Claim approved** *(pivotal — the money question is settled)*
7. Payout scheduled
8. Payout released
9. Claim closed

### Alternative & failure events

| Event | Branches from | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Claim rejected | 5 | rejection |
| Policy found lapsed | 3 | rejection |
| Additional evidence requested | 5 | human decision |
| Evidence deadline passed | after request | time passing |
| Claim escalated to fraud review | 4–5 | *(from the input)* |
| Claim cleared by fraud team | after escalation | human decision |
| Payout failed | 8 | external system |
| Payout recalled | after 8 | reversal |
| Claim reopened | after 9 | reversal |

### Slices

Everything in the Actor, Command, Aggregate, and Read model columns is
**inferred** — the input names none of them. Empty cells are deliberate.

| # | Actor / Policy | Command | Aggregate | Event | Read model |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer | Submit claim | Claim | Claim submitted | Policy summary |
| 2 | Customer | Attach photos | Claim | Photos attached to claim | |
| 3 | *policy:* whenever Claim submitted | Check policy validity | Policy | Policy validity checked | Policy record |
| 4 | *policy:* whenever Policy validity checked | Assign claim | Claim | Claim assigned to adjuster | Adjuster workload |
| 5 | Adjuster | Review claim | Claim | Claim reviewed | Claim file, Repair cost guide |
| 6 | Adjuster | Approve claim | Claim | Claim approved | Authority limits |
| 7 | *policy:* whenever Claim approved | Schedule payout | Payout | Payout scheduled | Payment details |
| 8 | Payment provider *(external)* | | Payout | Payout released | |
| 9 | *policy:* whenever Payout released | Close claim | Claim | Claim closed | |

Cross-context flow worth calling out: *Claim escalated to fraud review* suspends
the slice-4→5 chain rather than continuing it — a saga the board should draw
explicitly.

### Candidate bounded contexts

- **Intake** (1–2), **Underwriting/Policy** (3, Policy found lapsed),
  **Assessment** (4–6, evidence and fraud events), **Disbursement** (7–8, payout
  failures), **Case management** (9, Claim reopened).

### For the session

- **Assumptions to confirm:** that policy validation precedes assignment rather
  than running alongside; that the adjuster both reviews and approves (authority
  limits may split these); that fraud review is a suspension, not a parallel
  track.
- **Questions for the room:** what happens to the 5-day clock during fraud
  review? Can a rejected claim be appealed, and is that a new claim or a reopen?
  Who decides "enough evidence"?
- **Hotspots:** "within 5 days" is a service-level promise with no event marking
  its breach — candidate `Payout SLA breached`; the input's "approve or reject"
  hides a third real outcome, partial approval.

**What to notice:** the arrow "→ we check the policy is valid" became a *policy*
(automatic reaction), not an actor. The derivation rule did the work: nobody in
the description issues that command, so something must fire it.

## 3. Design-level seed: one aggregate lifecycle

**Input, verbatim:** *"Just the subscription itself — signup, monthly billing,
they can pause it, and it ends when they cancel or when payment fails too many
times."*

### Scope

The Subscription aggregate's lifecycle only. **Design-level** — one aggregate,
states and invariants included, no cross-context concerns.

### Event timeline (lifecycle, not strict chronology)

1. Subscription started
2. Trial period ended
3. Subscription renewed
4. Subscription paused
5. Subscription resumed
6. Payment attempt failed
7. Subscription suspended
8. Subscription reactivated
9. Subscription cancelled by customer
10. Subscription terminated for non-payment
11. Plan changed

### Slices

| Actor / Policy | Command | Event | Guard (invariant) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer | Start subscription | Subscription started | valid payment method on file |
| *policy:* on billing date | Renew subscription | Subscription renewed | status is Active |
| Customer | Pause subscription | Subscription paused | status is Active; pause budget not exhausted |
| Customer | Resume subscription | Subscription resumed | status is Paused |
| *policy:* on 3rd failure | Suspend subscription | Subscription suspended | consecutive failures ≥ 3 |
| Customer | Cancel subscription | Subscription cancelled by customer | status ≠ Terminated |

### States

`Trialing → Active ⇄ Paused`, `Active → Suspended → Active` (via reactivation) or
`Suspended → Terminated`, and `Active | Paused | Suspended → Cancelled`.
Terminal: **Cancelled**, **Terminated**.

### For the session

- **Assumptions to confirm:** the trial exists at all (the input never mentions
  it — inferred from "signup" preceding "monthly billing"); "too many times" = 3.
- **Questions for the room:** does a paused subscription still bill? Is
  cancellation immediate or at period end — the difference is an extra event,
  `Cancellation scheduled`. Can a terminated subscription be revived, or is that
  a new one?
- **Hotspots:** `Plan changed` (11) is unsequenced because the input never says
  when it's allowed — it may be forbidden while suspended.

**What to notice:** at this depth the timeline stops being a chronology and
becomes a lifecycle, guards appear, and the state list is the real deliverable.
The trial is flagged as invented rather than quietly included.

## 4. Naming clinic

| Given as | Reads as | Rename to | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Create order | a command | Order placed | intentions aren't facts |
| Order created | CRUD | Order placed | the business word is "placed" |
| User updated | CRUD | Delivery address corrected | name *what* changed and why |
| Processing payment | in-flight | Payment authorised | events are completed facts |
| Submit button clicked | UI | Claim submitted | the wall is not a screen |
| Record written to ledger | technical | Payment posted | domain language |
| Order handled | vague | Order picked · Order packed · Order dispatched | one vague event hides three real ones |
| Email sent | plumbing | Customer notified of dispatch | name the business intent, not the transport |
| Nightly job ran | mechanism | Overdue invoices flagged | the job is *how*; the flagging is *what* |
| Order not found | *legitimate* | Order not found | keep it — failure events are real; flag missing handling |
